Abstract—To address load imbalance in low earth orbit
(LEO) laser satellite networks (LSN), this paper proposes a
deep reinforcement learning (DRL) based routing algorithm,
which combines proximal policy optimization (PPO) and
K-shortest path (KSP) strategies to transform the large-scale
routing problem into a decision-making process over a small
set of paths. Simulation results demonstrate that, compared
with traditional Dijkstra and random routing algorithms, the
proposed algorithm fully exploits network resources,
effectively prevents network bottlenecks, and significantly
enhances the network’s service-carrying capacity.

I. INTRODUCTION
With the evolution of the Internet of Things and
sixth-generation (6G) technology, future networks must
support ultra-low latency, high capacity, and global coverage
[1]–[3]. Low earth orbit (LEO) satellite constellations,
particularly LEO laser satellite networks (LSNs) enabled by
laser
inter-satellite
links
(LISLs),
can
provide
high-bandwidth, low-interference communications that are
not subject to spectrum constraints [4]. However, uneven
spatiotemporal service distribution can lead to load
imbalance and link congestion, thereby limiting network
performance.
Traditional methods improve performance through
service-aware mechanisms such as dynamic link assignment,
time-scale load balancing, and backpressure routing [5]–[7],
as well as congestion-aware strategies [8], but they lack
adaptability. DRL-based methods, such as DDPG [9], SAC
[10], and DQN [11], can achieve adaptive routing, but suffer
from issues such as an overly large action space, low
decision accuracy, or training instability.
To address these issues, this paper proposes an algorithm
called KSP Enhanced PPO for Load-Balancing and
Congestion-Aware Routing (PPO-KSP), which uses KSP to
generate candidate paths, thereby reducing the action space,
while also leveraging PPO to learn adaptive routing
strategies under dynamic network conditions. In this way, the
proposed method improves service capacity, achieves
effective load balancing as well as congestion awareness.

B. Algorithm Flow
The PPO-KSP algorithm integrates the PPO algorithm
from DRL with the KSP algorithm from graph theory, the
latter being a classic method for computing multiple
candidate paths between nodes [12]. KSP reduces the
complexity of the action space, while PPO enables the
learning of optimal path selection under dynamic conditions.
The agent interacts with the environment, collects samples,
and updates its policy using truncated objective optimization
with generalized advantage estimation (GAE), thereby
ensuring stable convergence [13]. The overall procedure of

IV. SIMULATION SETTINGS AND RESULT ANALYSIS
A. Simulation Settings
This paper presents a Python-based simulation platform
for the Walker Delta satellite constellation. The constellation
consists of 42 satellites distributed across 6 orbital planes,
with 7 satellites in each plane. The DRL model is
implemented using PyTorch, and the proposed PPO-KSP
algorithm is systematically evaluated. In the comparative
experiments, to simulate dynamic arrivals, the traffic follows
a Poisson distribution with a density range of 900 to 2800, in
increments of 100. To ensure statistical reliability, each
algorithm undergoes 10 independent simulations at each
density level, processing 100,000 traffic instances per
simulation.
B. Simulation Result Analysis
As shown in Fig. 1, the value of K has a significant
impact on both convergence behavior and final performance.
When K=20 , the algorithm performs best, indicating an
appropriate balance between action diversity and training
stability. Smaller values of K limit the set of candidate paths,
thereby restricting the policy’s expressive power and leading
to less than optimal solutions. Conversely, larger values of K
expand the action space, which increases the variance of the
policy gradient estimates and slows down the convergence
rate.

Fig. 2 compares the performance of different routing

As shown in Fig. 2(a), the PPO-KSP algorithm achieves
the lowest BP across all traffic densities, indicating that it
significantly enhances network service capacity. This
improvement can be explained by the utilization
characteristics observed in Fig. 2(b) and Fig. 2(c).
Specifically, Fig. 2(b) shows that PPO-KSP maintains a
balanced ALU, demonstrating its ability to utilize network
bandwidth resources efficiently and uniformly. Compared to
the Dijkstra algorithm, which tends to concentrate traffic on
the shortest path and results in underutilization of other links,
PPO-KSP achieves more comprehensive utilization of
overall network resources. At the same time, compared to
random routing algorithms that distribute traffic randomly,
PPO-KSP avoids unbalanced resource allocation and
achieves more coordinated link utilization.
Fig. 2(c) further demonstrates that PPO-KSP effectively
suppresses excessive growth in link utilization, thereby
delaying link saturation. In contrast, the random routing
algorithm often leads to early congestion due to uncontrolled
traffic distribution, while the Dijkstra algorithm causes local
overload by concentrating traffic on a small set of shortest
paths.
