in LEO Satellite Networks

Abstract—In dynamic satellite networks, rapid topology
changes and resource constraints pose significant challenges for
efficient rerouting decisions. Traditional routing algorithms
struggle to adapt to the high-dynamic nature of Low Earth
Orbit (LEO) satellite constellations, while centralized deep
reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches suffer from high
communication overhead and privacy concerns. In this paper,
we propose a Federated Deep Q-Network (FedDQN) algorithm
that combines the decision-making capabilities of DRL with the
distributed training paradigm of federated learning. We
introduce three key contents: a semi-asynchronous federated
aggregation strategy that reduces idle waiting time by selecting
only a subset of active nodes per round; a Dueling Deep QNetwork (DQN) architecture that decomposes Q-values into
state-value and advantage functions for more accurate policy
evaluation; and a prioritized experience replay mechanism that
enhances sampling efficiency for critical network events.
Experimental results on a Walker Delta 288/12/1 constellation
demonstrate that FedDQN reduces cumulative communication
overhead by 70.8% compared to the standard DQN and by 61.0%
compared to Actor-Critic (A2C), while maintaining robust
convergence properties under various failure scenarios.

I. INTRODUCTION
Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite networks have emerged
as a critical infrastructure for global seamless communication,
offering advantages such as low transmission latency, widearea coverage, and rapid deployment capabilities[1]. However,
the inherent high-dynamic topology characteristics of LEO
networks, combined with stringent on-board resource
constraints, present formidable challenges for real-time and
effective rerouting algorithms. Specifically, the periodic
motion of satellite nodes leads to continuous network
topology changes and frequent link state updates, while
limited computational resources, storage capacity, and energy
supply on satellites hinder the execution of complex
algorithms in real-time[2].
Traditional routing protocols, such as OSPF and Dijkstra's
shortest path algorithm, rely on static topology information
and pre-computed routing tables, which are ill-suited for the
dynamic nature of satellite networks. These approaches lack
the adaptability to respond to rapid topology changes and
cannot optimize for multiple objectives such as latency,
reliability, and load balancing simultaneously[3].
Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) has shown
remarkable success in sequential decision-making tasks, with
Deep Q-Network(DQN) being one of the most representative
algorithms[4]. However, applying standard DQN directly to
LEO satellite network rerouting faces three fundamental
limitations: uniform random sampling from the experience

A. Federated Learning Framework
The Federated Learning framework for satellite networks
employs a "server-client" architecture, mapping the traditional
centralized FL paradigm onto the physical topology of the
satellite constellation. Within this architecture, cluster head
satellites serve as aggregation servers, responsible for
collecting, aggregating, and distributing global model
parameters. Regular satellite nodes act as clients, performing
model training using locally observed network state data and
uploading the resulting model parameters to the server.
The FL framework comprises three logical layers: the
Application Layer, the Coordination Layer, and the Execution
Layer. The Application Layer handles task scheduling, global
model initialization, and training result visualization. The
Coordination Layer, deployed on cluster head nodes, manages
local aggregation and inter-cluster communication
coordination. The Execution Layer, deployed on all satellite
nodes, executes local model training and parameter upload
operations. This layered architecture effectively decouples
global coordination from local computation, enhancing the
system's scalability.
The core advantage of the FL framework lies in the
principle of "data stays, model moves." Specifically, each
satellite node is required to upload only model parameters,
without transmitting raw network state data.

E. Prioritized Experience Replay
The Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) mechanism
enables importance-based non-uniform sampling by assigning
a priority score to each experience sample. When the agent
encounters unexpected state transitions or rewards, a large TD
error is generated, indicating that the current policy lacks
sufficient understanding of such scenarios and requires
repeated learning to update the value estimation. The PER
mechanism achieves optimal allocation of learning resources
by prioritizing the sampling of high-surprisal samples,
enabling limited training iterations to focus on the most
informative experiences.

2) Flow Generation Rules
Traffic flows are generated by first selecting source nodes
from a uniform probability distribution across all satellite
nodes, then randomly selecting destination nodes that satisfy
a strict 4-hop constraint from the corresponding source,
ensuring each end-to-end path traverses exactly four satelliteto-satellite links while maintaining randomness in spatial
distribution, with all flows sustained for the full 10-second
duration and packet payloads fixed at consistent sizes of 1,024
bytes.
3) Fault Injection Mechanism
To evaluate system resilience under dynamic failure
conditions, faults are deliberately injected at the temporal
midpoint of traffic generation at 5 seconds, specifically
comprising the random failure of 5% of the constellation (14
satellite nodes) coupled with the concurrent random
disruption of 10% of available inter-satellite links, thereby
simulating realistic degradation scenarios during active
network operation.
C. Simulation Framework Architecture
The simulation framework architecture comprises five
logical layers:
1) Infrastructure Layer
This layer is responsible for satellite network topology
generation and LEO routing environment simulation, thereby
providing the algorithms with a dynamic network state space
and interaction interfaces.
2) Agent Layer
This layer features independent implementations of DQN
and Advantage Actor-Critic (A2C), which execute routing
decisions based on value functions and policy gradients,
respectively. Specifically, the DQN module is subdivided into
three sub-components to incorporate the Dueling network
architecture and PER mechanism.
3) Training Architecture Layer
This layer encompasses the federated and centralized
modules; the former facilitates the federated aggregation and
distributed training processes of FedDQN, while the latter
enables comparative training for centralized DQN,
collectively supporting flexible switching between different
training paradigms.
4) Baseline Layer
This layer encapsulates the Dijkstra algorithm and A2C to
DQN policy conversion utilities within the baselines module,
establishing benchmark references for performance
evaluation. Composed of the utils and results modules along
with main.py, the Application Support Layer handles model
evaluation, visualization, and configuration management,
serving as the unified execution entry point. This modular
architecture effectively decouples environment simulation,
algorithm implementation, and training coordination.

V. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we present FedDQN, a federated Deep QNetwork for rerouting in LEO satellite networks. By
integrating Dueling DQN architecture with prioritized
experience replay within a semi-asynchronous federated
learning framework, FedDQN addresses the key challenges of
experience utilization, Q-value estimation accuracy, and
communication efficiency.
The experimental results demonstrate that FedDQN
achieves significant improvements over traditional and
existing learning-based approaches. FedDQN, through its
federated learning architecture, resolves the performance
bottleneck of standard DQN under increasing packet rates
(where standard DQN performance plateaus under high load),
while significantly outperforming A2C and Dijkstra baseline
algorithms.
Future work will focus on extending the approach to ultralarge constellations, handling heterogeneous fault scenarios
with coupled link and node failures, and implementing model
compression techniques for efficient on-board deployment.
