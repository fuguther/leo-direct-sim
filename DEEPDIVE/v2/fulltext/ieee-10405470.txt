Abstract—This paper provides a exploration of the
application of low earth orbit satellite networks (LSN) in the
context of sixth-generation(6G) mobile communication
technology. The primary focus of this research is to address
issues related to congestion control and routing within satellite
networks. We introduces ECSAC-DRO, a novel algorithm that
leverages deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to optimize
routing and congestion control. Experimental results
demonstrate that the ECSAC-DRO algorithm, taking into
consideration the high-dimensional characteristics of LSN and
incorporating elements from the DRL-based Soft Actor-Critic
(SAC) algorithm, excels in providing flexible control over LSN
congestion levels and bandwidth utilization. It outperforms
traditional methods like Dijkstra, making it a promising and
forward-looking solution that lends support to the future
development of 6G communication technology.

Another route for resolution involves adopting a
broadcasting method to seek optimal paths [8], but this
method leads to substantial resource and energy wastage.
Furthermore, the extensive routing calculations associated
with LEO constellations make convergence challenging. In
response to these challenges, researchers have explored
strategies such as employing the nearest neighbor propagation
clustering algorithm to solve the problem of uneven
distribution of ground users, fluctuating reliability of nodes
and links, and frequent switching of controller groups [9].
They have also explored heuristic algorithms tailored to
specific regions, aiming to mitigate congestion issues [10].
However, these methods heavily rely on manual configuration
and exhibit limited robustness, hindering their widespread
adoption.

Keywords—Congestion control, LEO satellite networks
routing, deep reinforcement learning

I. INTRODUCTION
The advent of 5G has advanced the Internet of Things, but
challenges persist, prompting anticipation for 6G [1]. 5G's
network communication is limited by hardware, and its global
deployment demands substantial resources. 6G strives for
global coverage with a satellite network focus, making
communication technology and integration its central research
concerns [2].

In recent years, the field of machine learning has
experienced a rapid evolution, with DRL emerging as a
convergence of deep learning's robust cognitive capabilities
and reinforcement learning's decision-making prowess. DRL
empowers intelligent agents to explore their surroundings
within predefined contexts, quantifying the consequences of
their actions and assessing their navigational outcomes. This
process culminates in the formulation of strategies for optimal
decision-making in complex environments. Consequently,
DRL has found broad-ranging applications across domains
like natural language processing, computer vision, and
autonomous driving[11]-[13]. Likewise, DRL holds the
potential to make significant contributions in the realm of
LEO route optimization.[14] turned to a DQN-based
algorithm to tackle the load balancing issues within LEO
routing networks. However, a drawback of this approach is the
discrete action space, which limits its capacity to faithfully
represent real-world challenges.What’s more, [15] ventured
into the realm of using a DDPG-based algorithm to combat
the issue of excessive energy consumption in LSN.
Employing a continuous action space, this innovative
approach offers a fresh perspective on alleviating routing
congestion within LSN. Nevertheless, DDPG's performance
in high-dimensional state spaces left much room for

The existing satellite orbits primarily comprise Low Earth
Orbit (LEO), Medium Earth Orbit (MEO), and
Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (GTO). As technology
continues to advance, the success rate of satellite launches is
continuously improving, costs are decreasing, and LEO orbit
resources are more abundant [3]. However, to fully realize
their potential, LEO orbits require a large number of satellites
to form satellite constellations. In recent years, the
development of large-scale constellation networks has been
rapid. As these networks continue to evolve, the issues they
face are gradually turning into pain points. Specifically, due to
geographical, political, and other factors, congestion near the
traffic entry and exit points of constellation routing networks
has become a serious concern. This congestion is increasingly
becoming a bottleneck for the overall network and may have
disastrous consequences for the entire network
infrastructure[4]-[6].

A. Experimental Environment Configuration
We have developed a Python-based computational
environment for satellite constellation network routing.
Additionally, we have constructed a DRL algorithm model
using Python and conducted an extensive series of simulation
evaluations for the ECSAC-DRO algorithm. The experiments
were performed on a network with a satellite position matrix
randomly generated, consisting of 20 satellites. We
established random connection relationships and transmitted
50 data packets. Prior to exploration, each generated network
underwent an assessment to determine its validity, ensuring
that bad cases had minimal impact on the experiments.The
primary goal of the DRL exploration in this study is to select
routing paths for data packet transmission based on congestion
levels and average bandwidth utilization. The aim is to
optimize network congestion and average bandwidth
utilization, striving to reach an ideal level.

B. Analysis of Experimental Results

In our routing management system, we utilize the SAC
algorithm from DRL. SAC combines deep neural networks
and policy gradient methods to facilitate learning and
optimization. It excels in handling not only continuous state
and action spaces but also offers improved convergence
properties compared to conventional Q-learning methods.
